KSP FIR SYSTEM — GLOSSARY AND CASE LIFECYCLE
(Reference knowledge for the KSP Intelligent Crime Analytics Chatbot)

1. CASE CATEGORIES (CaseCategory table)
The Karnataka Police system registers cases under four categories, each identified
by a 1-digit prefix in the CrimeNo:
- FIR (First Information Report) — prefix 1 — registered when a cognizable offence
  is reported; this is the standard case type that triggers a full investigation.
- UDR (Unnatural Death Report) — prefix 3 — registered when a death occurs under
  unnatural or unexplained circumstances and it is not yet clear whether a crime
  was committed.
- Zero FIR — prefix 8 — an FIR registered at any police station regardless of
  jurisdiction, to be transferred later to the station with actual jurisdiction.
  Used so a victim is never turned away for filing at the "wrong" station.
- PAR (Preliminary/Property/Arrest Report, station-specific usage) — prefix 4 —
  used for cases that do not require a full FIR but still need to be logged.

2. CRIME NUMBER (CrimeNo) FORMAT
CrimeNo is an 18-digit code with no separators:
  [1-digit Case Category] + [4-digit District ID] + [4-digit Police Station/Unit ID]
  + [4-digit Year] + [5-digit running serial number]
Example: 104430006202600001
  1     = FIR
  0443  = District ID
  0006  = Police Station (Unit) ID
  2026  = Year
  00001 = Serial number (resets per station, per category, per year)

CaseNo is simply the last 9 digits of CrimeNo (Year + 5-digit serial), e.g. 202600001.

3. GRAVITY OF OFFENCE (GravityOffence table)
Every case is classified as:
- Heinous — serious offences (e.g. murder, rape, dacoity) that receive priority
  investigation attention and stricter monitoring.
- Non-Heinous — lesser offences (e.g. petty theft, minor disputes).

4. CASE STATUS (CaseStatusMaster table)
A case moves through these statuses over its lifecycle:
- Under Investigation — active, investigating officer is still gathering evidence.
- Charge Sheeted — investigation complete, a chargesheet has been filed in court.
- Closed — case concluded (chargesheeted-and-disposed, or closed as false/undetected).

5. CHARGESHEET FINAL REPORT TYPES (ChargesheetDetails.cstype)
When an investigation concludes, the final report is classified as one of:
- A = Chargesheet — sufficient evidence found, case sent to court for trial.
- B = False Case — investigation determined the complaint was false/unfounded.
- C = Undetected — investigation could not identify the offender(s); case remains open
  in records but no chargesheet is filed.

6. PEOPLE INVOLVED IN A CASE
- Complainant — the person who reported the incident to police (ComplainantDetails).
- Victim — the person who suffered harm (Victim); may or may not be the same as
  the complainant. Victims are tagged with GenderID values m/f/t.
- Accused — the person(s) alleged to have committed the offence (Accused).
  Each accused gets a display code A1, A2, A3... (PersonID) for readability in
  reports and charts. GenderID for accused uses M/F/T (uppercase).
- Investigating Officer (IO) — the Employee assigned to investigate; recorded via
  PolicePersonID on CaseMaster and IOID on ArrestSurrender.

7. ARREST VS SURRENDER
An accused person is linked to a case via an ArrestSurrender event, which records:
- Whether they were arrested by police or voluntarily surrendered.
- The date, and the state/district/police station where it happened.
- Which court they were produced before.
- Two flags: IsAccused (are they the primary accused in this case) and
  IsComplainantAccused (is the original complainant also named as an accused,
  e.g. in cross-cases).
One arrest/surrender event can, through a junction table, link to multiple accused
persons (e.g. a group arrest).

8. ACTS AND SECTIONS
Every case can invoke one or more legal Act + Section combinations (e.g. "IPC
Section 302"), recorded in ActSectionAssociation with a display order for
printing on the FIR copy. Acts (e.g. IPC, NDPS, BNS) are the parent law; Sections
are the specific numbered provisions within that act. Crime heads (major
categories like "Crimes Against Body") and crime sub-heads (specific crime types
like "Murder", "Robbery") are separately classified and linked to typical
act-sections via a mapping table, for classification/reporting purposes.

9. GEOGRAPHY AND ORGANIZATION HIERARCHY
State → District → Unit (police station / circle office, has a parent-unit
hierarchy) → Employee (posted with a Rank such as Constable/Inspector/DSP and
a Designation such as Investigating Officer/SHO). Courts are also linked to a
District and State, and cases/arrests reference the specific court handling them.
